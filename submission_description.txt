Project Title: Data Visualization and Insight Communication Using R
Selected Dataset: Kaggle Superstore Sales Dataset (9,994 records, 21 columns, 209,874 cells)
Internship Track: Data Analytics & Science (Week 2 Final Overhaul Submission)
GitHub URL: https://github.com/248r1a6767-byte/yuvainternweek2

This submission delivers an end-to-end, publication-grade data analytics and data visualization project executed entirely in R (version 4.6.1) utilizing ggplot2, dplyr, tidyr, lubridate, scales, forcats, and patchwork. The analytical investigation evaluates 9,994 commercial line-item transactions spanning four continuous calendar years (January 2011 to December 2014) across 49 US states, generating $2,297,200.86 in gross revenue and $286,397.02 in net operating profit (12.47% commercial margin).

The analytical pipeline comprises 8 modular, fully reproducible R scripts orchestrated by a master runner (scripts/run_all.R). A suite of 14 publication-grade figures was engineered at 300 DPI resolution adhering strictly to the Grammar of Graphics and Cleveland-McGill perceptual hierarchy. Crucially, addressing evaluator feedback, EVERY single visualization in the comprehensive Word report embeds the exact, runnable R ggplot2 code block, an accompanying supporting numerical evidence table with exact values and percentages, and a structured three-tier analytical narrative: (1) WHAT? (concrete empirical observations), (2) SO WHAT? (statistical and operational significance), and (3) NOW WHAT? (actionable C-suite strategic recommendations).

Key empirical insights communicated across the visual and tabular portfolio include:
1. Category Profitability Asymmetry: While Technology serves as the primary growth engine ($836,154.03 sales, $145,454.95 profit at 17.39% margin, capturing 50.79% of company profit), Furniture presents an acute operational crisis. Furniture absorbs 32.30% of sales ($741,999.80) but returns a meager $18,451.27 in profit (6.44% profit share, 2.49% margin). Granular sub-category divergence plots pinpoint Tables (-$17,725.48 net loss, -8.56% margin) and Bookcases (-$3,472.56 net loss) as systemic financial drains.
2. The 20% Promotional Discount Cliff: Non-linear regression and binned performance audits confirm that discounts <= 20% yield $421,773.08 in profit (21.80% margin across 8,601 items), whereas discounts > 20% destroy -$135,376.06 across 1,393 line items. At discounts > 50%, 99.88% of transactions lose money (Spearman rank rho = -0.5434, p < 0.0001).
3. Geographic Disparities & Discounting Discipline: The West region leads enterprise profitability ($108,418.45 profit, 14.94% margin) through strict discount discipline (10.93% average discount). Conversely, the Central region suffers severe margin compression (7.92% margin, $39,706.36 profit) due to excessive discounting (24.04% average). State-level divergence analysis highlights California (+$76.4K) and New York (+$74.0K) as twin profit engines, while Texas (-$25.7K at 37.0% discount) and Ohio (-$17.0K at 32.5% discount) inflict massive structural deficits.
4. Customer Segments & Logistics SLAs: Corporate and Home Office segments demonstrate superior margin efficiency (13.03% and 14.03%) compared to retail Consumers (11.55%). Fulfillment boxplots verify 97.8% overall logistics SLA compliance (Standard Class averaging 5.01 days).
